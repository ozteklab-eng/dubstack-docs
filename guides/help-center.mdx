Use this guide when your docs site should help customers solve product questions without contacting support.

A help center is different from a developer portal. It prioritizes reader intent, common tasks, and clear support escalation over implementation detail.

## Start from support themes

Collect the most repeated questions from:

- support conversations
- sales handoffs
- onboarding calls
- feedback analytics
- search terms with poor results
- assistant questions that do not resolve cleanly

Group pages by what readers are trying to do, not by your internal team structure.

## Recommended structure

| Group | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Getting started | Setup, account creation, first success path. |
| Account and billing | Plans, invoices, users, access, and limits. |
| Product guides | Task-based guides for common outcomes. |
| Troubleshooting | Known problems, error states, recovery steps. |
| Contact support | Escalation paths and what details to include. |

Keep pages short. A reader should know within a few seconds whether the page answers their question.

## Write answer-first pages

For each page:

1. Start with the answer or outcome.
2. List prerequisites.
3. Provide numbered steps.
4. Add common problems.
5. Link to related pages.

Avoid burying the resolution under background explanation.

## Use feedback loops

Enable reader feedback when you want visitors to report missing or confusing content.

Review feedback alongside:

- page views
- failed searches
- assistant conversations
- workflow suggestions

Use the feedback workflow to draft focused improvements from repeated issues.

## Connect support context carefully

Integrations can help workflows use support themes as context, but support data can contain sensitive information.

Before connecting support systems:

- confirm the provider is approved for your workspace
- scope access to the minimum required data
- avoid copying private customer data into public docs
- review generated drafts before publishing
